FIG. 01 · Meet AX-1

A robot whose brain is the phone you already own

Why a phone?

A robot needs a brain with senses, power and a connection. You already carry one. AX-1 uses your phone for the hard parts; the body only adds motors and a small safety bridge.

  • It sees and hears. Cameras, a microphone and a speaker are already there.
  • It feels movement. Motion sensors and GPS tell the robot how it moves and where it is.
  • It thinks on the device. A fast processor, with an AI chip on most recent phones, runs the control software locally.
  • It has a face and a link. The screen is AX-1's face; Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and its own battery are built in.
  • It keeps getting better. A software update upgrades the brain without touching the body.
FIG. 02 · Questions

FAQ

Which phone works as AX-1's brain?

Any phone drives AX-1's 3D twin. To pair with a real robot (once you've built one) you need Android Chrome: it has Bluetooth in the browser. iPhone Safari doesn't, so iPhones stay with the twin. An old phone is fine.

What is a twin?

A twin is the 3D copy of a robot that you drive in your browser. Twins are labelled TWIN, real build photos BUILD. Today, everything you drive is a twin.

Is the robot safe to build?

The bridge firmware holds every joint still if the phone goes quiet, checks joint limits and speed itself, and needs a deliberate "arm" from you before moving again. Every build also has a physical power switch. The firmware is experimental and untested on hardware until our first build: start with the wheels off the ground, and build with an adult.

How do I mint an agent?

Only on OpenSea, through our drop page. Price: 0.01024 ETH for everyone (the allowlist only mints earlier), max 3 per wallet, plus network fees; the Market page tracks the collection. You need a wallet app such as MetaMask or Rabby. Never type your recovery phrase anywhere: the team will never ask for it, and we never DM first. Buying an agent involves crypto and is for adults.

Is this an investment?

No. We promise no returns or profit. An agent is a collectible with tools, and its price can fall as well as rise. Only spend what you'd be fine not getting back.

Can I use the art and the designs commercially?

Yes, and so can anyone: everything we make is CC0. Third-party designs we link to keep their own licenses, and some are non-commercial. About CC0 →

FIG. 04 · What an agent (NFT) is

An agent is an NFT: your membership and logbook in the lab

  • What it is. A profile you own: a robot class, a wallet and a mission record. It is not a robot you can drive, and no agent software ships yet.
  • 11 classes. AX-1 Rover, Desk and Walker (31% of the collection), plus 8 inspired by open platforms. In the gallery they appear as Synth Arm (SO-101 arm), Street Rover (OpenBot rover), Cyber Hound (quadruped), Ghost Hand (hand), Spider Mech (hexapod), Sky Drone (drone), Fab Rig (3D printer) and Replicant (humanoid).
  • Missions with a record. In the Proving Grounds your agent flies its own robot and the three AX-1 bodies. Each passed run is replayed by a verifier and written to the blockchain, so nobody can self-report a score. It is lab-simulation data, not proof of a real robot.
  • Its own wallet. Every agent has an Ethereum wallet of its own (a standard called ERC-6551), created at mint. Whoever holds the agent controls it. Sell the agent and the wallet goes with it.
  • Autonomy you set. It starts in Manual: it proposes, you approve. Assisted and Autonomous will act only within a daily limit you choose; the agent software for them isn't out yet. An emergency pause is one click away, and before selling you can lock the wallet for up to 30 days: until then nobody, you included, can make it send anything, so a buyer knows it can't be emptied through it (they get full control when the lock ends; approvals it gave other apps earlier still count, so check them before buying). It can never move your other tokens.
  • Real robots next. Robot Link replays a run on a real SO-101 arm; the Phone Brain is built to drive a real AX-1, but the firmware is untested on hardware until our first build. No real-robot records or wallet history exist yet.
  • Dormant, then revealed. At mint you get a dormant agent. After the sale ends, a public random draw assigns traits, so nobody can pick the rare ones early. The art is drawn from the lab's 3D models and is CC0: anyone may use it.

Why hold an agent

Beyond the record and the wallet above, an agent gives you a seat in how the lab grows. What exists today, and what is planned:

  • Today Sell what you build. Everything is CC0: your kits, prints and guides are yours to sell, and your agent's record shows what you have done.
  • In development Credited datasets. Every verified run is already hashed and tied to your agent; the next step is open robot-learning datasets that credit each contributor.
  • Planned Prize missions. Seasonal Proving Grounds events with prizes paid to the winners' agent wallets. The missions, verifier and leaderboards already run.
  • Planned Builder bounties. Paid tasks to build, test and document AX-1 and the other robots, paid by the lab's treasury to your agent.
  • Planned Early access and votes. First access to new bodies and tools, and a holder vote on what the lab builds next.

Planned items are intentions, not commitments: they may change or never ship. Any earning would come from what you do (a prize, a bounty, a kit you sell), never from holding. No revenue share, no dividends, no promise of value. The 5% resale royalty funds the lab's treasury.
