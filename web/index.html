FIG. 01 · Getting started

Four steps, from curious to builder

  1. Learn. Open the Workbench: every robot explained in clickable 3D stages. Take it apart, see its sensors and its brain.
  2. Test. Play each robot's missions in the browser. Three levels each, scored by an honest lab sim.
  3. Build. Pick an AX-1 body: Rover (wheels and a head), Desk (a neck and two arms) or Walker (a community build). Parts list, wiring and firmware are all open.
  4. Pilot. Open the Phone Brain on your phone. No robot yet? It drives AX-1's 3D twin, or puts the twin in your room (AR, on a phone) to drive by voice.
FIG. 02 · Meet AX-1

A robot whose brain is the phone you already own

  • The phone thinks. Its camera, motion sensors and processor do the seeing and deciding. AX-1's face is on its screen.
  • A small board moves. An ESP32 bridge drives the motors and servos and keeps them safe. If the phone goes quiet, AX-1 stops and holds.
  • Open and CC0. Models, firmware, app and docs are public domain. Build it, change it, sell kits: no permission needed.

Why a phone?

A robot needs a brain with senses, power and a connection. You already carry one. AX-1 uses your phone for the hard parts; the body only adds motors and a small safety bridge.

  • It sees and hears. Cameras, a microphone and a speaker are already there.
  • It feels movement. Motion sensors and GPS tell the robot how it moves and where it is.
  • It thinks on the device. A fast processor, with an AI chip on most recent phones, runs the control software locally.
  • It has a face and a link. The screen is AX-1's face; Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and its own battery are built in.
  • It keeps getting better. A software update upgrades the brain without touching the body.

Today Phone Brain drives AX-1's 3D twin, and its AI Pilot turns typed or spoken requests into safe moves. Planned The same app driving a real AX-1 by touch, text or voice, then on-phone vision for the Assisted and Autonomous modes. The bridge firmware is experimental until our first hardware build.

FIG. 04 · Questions

FAQ

What does an agent add to the lab?

Anyone can explore the lab as a guest. An agent (your NFT) is what turns that time into something you own: its own wallet, a public record of your verified missions under its number, and autonomy settings you control. Guests play every mission too, but their runs are not saved to any record.

Which phone works as AX-1's brain?

Any phone drives AX-1's 3D twin. To pair with a real robot (once you've built one) you need Android Chrome: it has Bluetooth in the browser. iPhone Safari doesn't, so iPhones stay with the twin. An old phone is fine.

What do I need to build a real AX-1?

Your phone, a small ESP32 bridge board, motors or bus servos, a battery and printed parts. The Rover uses two geared motors and two head servos; the Desk uses seven bus servos. The Walker is a longer community project. The Workbench and the build guide list every part and its open source.

What works today, and what is still to come?

Works today: the Workbench (11 robots), missions scored by a lab simulation, the Phone Brain driving AX-1's 3D twin, open build guides. Not yet: a filmed AX-1 on real hardware, real-robot run records, and agent software for Assisted and Autonomous modes. The AX-1 firmware is experimental.

What is a twin?

A twin is the 3D copy of a robot that you drive in your browser. Twins are labelled TWIN, real build photos BUILD. Today, everything you drive is a twin.

Is the robot safe to build?

The bridge firmware holds every joint still if the phone goes quiet, checks joint limits and speed itself, and needs a deliberate "arm" from you before moving again. Every build also has a physical power switch. The firmware is experimental until our first hardware build: start with the wheels off the ground.

Is it OK for kids or a classroom?

The lab needs no account, wallet or payment. The Phone Brain uses the camera only inside your browser; nothing is recorded or sent. Buying an agent involves crypto and is for adults. Build the robot with an adult: the firmware is experimental, so start with the wheels off the ground.

How do I mint an agent?

Only on OpenSea, through our drop page. Price: 0.015 ETH (0.01 ETH on the allowlist), max 3 per wallet, plus network fees. You need a wallet app such as MetaMask or Rabby. At mint you receive a dormant agent; its class and traits are revealed after the sale. Never type your recovery phrase anywhere: the team will never ask for it.

Is this an investment?

No. We promise no returns or profit. An agent is a collectible with tools, and its price can fall as well as rise. Only spend what you'd be fine not getting back.

Can I use the art and the designs commercially?

Yes, and so can anyone: everything we make is CC0. Third-party designs we link to keep their own licenses, and some are non-commercial. About CC0 →

FIG. 06 · What an agent (NFT) is

An agent is an NFT: your membership and logbook in the lab

  • What it is. A profile you own: a robot class, a wallet and a mission record. It is not a robot you can drive, and no agent software ships yet.
  • 11 classes. AX-1 Rover, Desk and Walker (31% of the collection), plus 8 inspired by open platforms. In the gallery they appear as Synth Arm (SO-101 arm), Street Rover (OpenBot rover), Cyber Hound (quadruped), Ghost Hand (hand), Spider Mech (hexapod), Sky Drone (drone), Fab Rig (3D printer) and Replicant (humanoid).
  • Missions with a record. In the Proving Grounds your agent flies its own robot and the three AX-1 bodies. Each passed run is replayed by a verifier and written to the blockchain, so nobody can self-report a score. It is lab-simulation data, not proof of a real robot.
  • Its own wallet. Every agent has an Ethereum wallet of its own (a standard called ERC-6551), created at mint. Whoever holds the agent controls it. Sell the agent and the wallet goes with it.
  • Autonomy you set. It starts in Manual: it proposes, you approve. Assisted and Autonomous will act only within a daily limit you choose; the agent software for them isn't out yet. An emergency pause is one click away, and before selling you can lock the wallet for up to 30 days: until then nobody, you included, can make it send anything, so a buyer knows it can't be emptied through it (they get full control when the lock ends; approvals it gave other apps earlier still count, so check them before buying). It can never move your other tokens.
  • Real robots next. Robot Link replays a run on a real SO-101 arm; the Phone Brain is built to drive a real AX-1, but the firmware is untested on hardware until our first build. No real-robot records or wallet history exist yet.
  • Dormant, then revealed. At mint you get a dormant agent. After the sale ends, a public random draw assigns traits, so nobody can pick the rare ones early. The art is drawn from the lab's 3D models and is CC0: anyone may use it.

Not an investment: no returns promised, and the price can fall. Mint only on OpenSea; we never DM first.

Why hold an agent

An agent is your place in the lab: a record of what you do in it, a wallet of its own, and a seat in how the lab grows. What exists today, and what is planned:

  • Today A verified record. Passed and failed missions are replayed by the verifier and written on-chain under your agent's number.
  • Today Its own wallet, with limits you set. Approve each action, set a daily limit, pause it, lock it before selling.
  • Today Sell what you build. Everything is CC0: your kits, prints and guides are yours to sell, and your agent's record shows what you have done.
  • In development Credited datasets. Every verified run is already hashed and tied to your agent; the next step is open robot-learning datasets that credit each contributor.
  • Planned Prize missions. Seasonal Proving Grounds events with prizes paid to the winners' agent wallets. The missions, verifier and leaderboards already run.
  • Planned Builder bounties. Paid tasks to build, test and document AX-1 and the other robots, paid by the lab's treasury to your agent.
  • Planned Early access and votes. First access to new bodies and tools, and a holder vote on what the lab builds next.
  • In development Assisted and Autonomous modes. The agent wallet already supports them; the agent software that uses them is not released yet.

Planned items are intentions, not commitments: they may change or never ship. Any earning would come from what you do (a prize, a bounty, a kit you sell), never from holding. No revenue share, no dividends, no promise of value. The 5% resale royalty funds the lab's treasury.

Can I earn with my agent?

Not by holding it. Planned prize missions and bounties would pay for work you do, and you can already sell your own CC0 builds. None of it is guaranteed.

Do holders get a share of revenue or royalties?

No. The 5% resale royalty goes to the lab's treasury, which pays for hardware, development, prizes and bounties. There is no revenue share or dividend.

Will my agent go up in value?

We make no claim about price. The Market page shows sales and holders for information only. Buy an agent only if you want the record and the access, and only with money you can afford to lose.
