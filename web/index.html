FIG. 01 · Getting started

Four steps, from curious to builder

  1. Learn. Open the Workbench: every robot explained in clickable 3D stages. Take it apart, see its sensors and its brain.
  2. Test. Play each robot's missions in the browser. Three levels each, scored by an honest lab sim.
  3. Build. Pick an AX-1 body: Rover (about $100–115), Desk (about $150–170) or Walker (a community build). Parts list, wiring and firmware are all open.
  4. Pilot. Open the Phone Brain on your phone. No robot yet? It drives AX-1's 3D twin.
FIG. 02 · Meet AX-1

A robot whose brain is the phone you already own

  • The phone thinks. Its camera, motion sensors and processor do the seeing and deciding. AX-1's face is on its screen.
  • A small board moves. An ESP32 bridge drives the motors and servos and keeps them safe. If the phone goes quiet, AX-1 stops and holds.
  • Open and CC0. Models, firmware, app and docs are public domain. Build it, change it, sell kits: no permission needed.

FIG. 04 · Questions

FAQ

Do I need an NFT to use the lab?

No. The Workbench, the missions, the Phone Brain and every build guide are free and open to everyone. An agent adds an on-chain identity, a wallet and a public record of your runs.

Which phone works as AX-1's brain?

An Android phone with Chrome connects to the robot over Bluetooth. iPhones can't use Web Bluetooth in Safari, so they drive the 3D twin (or use a Bluetooth-capable browser). An old phone is fine.

What does a real AX-1 cost?

Without the phone: about $100–115 for the Rover and $150–170 for the Desk body, from parts you can order online. The Walker is a longer community project. Prices change; the build guide lists each part and its source.

Is the robot safe to build?

The bridge firmware holds every joint still if the phone goes quiet, checks joint limits and speed itself, and needs a deliberate "arm" from you before moving again. Every build also has a physical power switch. The firmware is experimental until our first hardware build: start with the wheels off the ground.

How do I mint an agent?

Only on OpenSea, through its drop page: connect a wallet such as MetaMask or Rabby and press Mint. Never type your 12-word recovery phrase anywhere: the team will never ask for it.

Is this an investment?

No. OpenForge AX promises no returns or profits. Agents are digital objects with lab utility, and their market value can go up or down.

Can I use the art and the designs commercially?

Yes, and so can anyone: everything we make is CC0. Third-party designs we link to keep their own licenses, and some are non-commercial. About CC0 →

FIG. 06 · What an agent does

An agent is a robot's identity in the lab, with its own wallet and record

  • A robot class. 11 classes: AX-1 Rover, Desk and Walker (31% of the collection), plus 8 classes inspired by open platforms (SO-101 arm, OpenBot rover, quadruped, hand, hexapod, drone, 3D printer, humanoid).
  • Missions with a record. In the Proving Grounds your agent flies its own robot and the three AX-1 bodies. A passed run is replayed by the verifier and attested on-chain, so the record can't be faked. It is lab data, not proof of a real robot.
  • Its own wallet. Every agent owns an ERC-6551 account, created at mint. Only the current holder controls it: top it up or withdraw, from the agent's page.
  • Autonomy you set. It starts in Manual: it proposes, you approve. Assisted and Autonomous work only within a daily limit you choose. An emergency pause and a 7-day sale lock are one click away. It can never move your other tokens.
  • Real robots next. Robot Link replays a run on a real SO-101 arm; the Phone Brain drives a real AX-1 (experimental firmware). Records of real-robot runs are on the roadmap.
  • Open art and traits. Traits are computed on-chain from a seed drawn after the sale. The art is drawn from the lab's 3D models, and everything is CC0.

Not an investment: no returns are promised, and the lab is free for everyone, with or without an agent. Mint only on OpenSea.
